# Exposure Scale

A guided self-check of whether a person's guardrails keep up with how they use AI. It places them on five levels (Ask, Work with, Delegate, Automate, Hand over), asks what they do at each level, and shows how many of their guardrails are in place and what to fix first. Live at https://yaedin.github.io/exposure-scale/ (GitHub Pages, from `main`, root).

Goal of this stage: show that the idea works (people learn something they didn't know and act on it), not full coverage.

**Why cover, not level (Nov 2026).** The first version put the level at the centre: three questions placed you, and the gap between your guess and your answers was the payoff. Tests with about ten people showed that people estimate their level well, so the result told them little, and that copy which read as AI-written cost trust. This version takes the level in one question and spends the rest on what people can't see from the inside: whether the guardrails of each level are in place. The scale now answers three things: (a) how many of your guardrails are in place, (b) what to fix first, (c) what the next level needs before you move up.

**One level, one count (Nov 2026).** The first build of this version asked two level questions (most weeks, and anything further last month) and called each level covered, partly covered or open. The owner found the screens confusing: they named three level numbers at once (the level a question checks, the weekly level, the tried level) and needed a definition of "covered". Now one question asks for the furthest level in the last month, even once, because the risks start the first time. The page names one number ("You go up to level 4") and counts guardrails ("3 of your 8 guardrails are in place"). Don't bring back a second level number or state words for levels.

**Scope: AI only.** General cybersecurity (passwords, sign-in, updates, credit freezes, data brokers) was cut in November 2026 after feedback that the start felt like standard privacy advice; it's in the git history. Don't add it back without asking. A step belongs here if AI creates or changes the risk.

## Ground rules
- **No build step.** Code and styles live in `index.html`, content in `content.json`, fonts in `fonts/`. The page fetches `content.json`, so preview it through a local server (`python3 -m http.server`), not by opening the file.
- **No outside requests.** No CDNs, analytics, embeds or Google Fonts. Answers stay in `localStorage` only. The footer's privacy note depends on this; update it if anything changes.
- **Guide, don't display.** One clear answer, then one next step. No tabs, tallies or dashboards. This comes from the design brief of the owner's personal site, which links here.
- **Lean copy that a person would write.** Short, plain sentences. No defensive caveats, no filler, no cheer ("Good instinct"), no two-beat slogans, no full stop at the end of a heading. The owner explicitly dislikes verbose text, and testers lost trust where copy read as AI-written.
- **Every number has a source.** A note or case that states a number or names an incident carries `src`, the id of an entry in `sources`, and the page links it. `tools/check_content.py` fails on a note with a number and no source. Don't add a claim you haven't opened the source for.
- **The count must be fair.** The result counts essential steps in place, so every essential guardrail needs an answer option that can mark it `done`. `tools/check_content.py` fails otherwise. Without this, nobody could reach a full count by answering honestly, and the result would read as rigged.
- **Comfort first.** One question per screen, number-key shortcuts, instant feedback after each answer. Single choice advances after a short pause that shows the pick; multi choice waits for Continue (with a count), which sticks to the bottom on phones. A screen animates in only when it is new, never on a redraw of the same one.
- **Use the design tokens** (see Design system below). No new literal colors, font sizes, radii or control heights.

## Design system
The design follows the owner's personal site (direction "Guide", design brief of 12 November 2026): plain ground, one sans-serif, generous space, brown lines, a green wash. All tokens sit in `:root` at the top of the style block in `index.html`. Adding a value means adding a token and a line here, not a one-off.
- **Color:** `--bg` (plain ground, never tinted), three text shades `--ink` (headings, labels you act on), `--text` (body), `--muted` (meta; dark enough to read, never light grey), one hairline `--line`, one `--accent` (brown) and one `--wash` (green). Dark values override the same names. Color marks only what you can do or what is in place: links, buttons, option markers and ticks are accent; the wash fills what is in place or chosen. There are no status colors; a level's state shows in the shape of its node and in words.
- **The wash:** `.wash` paints a rough-edged fill behind an element through the SVG filter `#wash` at the top of `<body>` (the same filter as on the personal site). Used for the primary button, a chosen option, a ticked step and the node of a level with guardrails in place (half the node when only some are).
- **Theme:** one icon button in the header cycles Auto (half circle, follows the system) → Light (sun) → Dark (moon); the icon shows the current state, the tooltip and label name it and the next one. Stored under `exposure-scale-theme` (Start over keeps it) and applied by a small script in `<head>` before paint.
- **Type:** one family, IBM Plex Sans, weights 400/500/600; headings are 500. Six sizes: `--t-xs` 14px (labels, meta, footer), `--t-sm` 16px (buttons, links, secondary text), `--t-md` 18px (body), `--t-lg` 21px (level names, the first step), `--t-xl` 26–32px (questions), `--t-2xl` 32–44px (the statement on the start and result screens). One tracking, `--tight`, for `h1`. Sentence case everywhere: no monospace, no all-caps, no full stop at the end of a heading. Numbers that should line up get `.num` (tabular figures). The personal site uses Schibsted Grotesk; this page stays on IBM Plex Sans until the owner decides to add that font file.
- **Shape:** `--r-bar` 3px (the progress segments), `--r-ctl` 6px (buttons, the prompt box, focus rings); markers, ticks and nodes are circles. `--w-line` 1.5px for every drawn line: circle outlines, button borders, the rail. Hairlines between sections are 1px `--line`. No cards, boxes with fills, gradients or shadows.
- **Sizes:** `--h-ctl` 46px (buttons, text links as tap targets, the theme button), `--h-node` 48px (level nodes; also the minimum height of an option row), `--h-mark` 28px (option markers and step ticks), `--h-icon` 20px (icons).
- **Layout:** `.cols` puts `.lead` (the statement) left and `.side` (the rail) right on wide screens and stacks them on phones. Text blocks stop at `34rem`.

## How it works
- `content.json` holds all content: `intro` (start screen `title` and `line`, the "Use your AI" copy, the footer line `by`, the `basis` paragraph), `levels`, `around` (risks at every level), `sources`, `parts`, `steps`, `questions`, and `linksChecked` (shown in the footer).
- `levels`: `n`, `name`, `line` (what it looks like), `up` (how to get there), `risk` (what it adds), `case` (`{text, src}`: one real incident or number, linked to its source), `echo` (optional `{question, text}`: repeats the person's own answer to that question inside the level, e.g. what their agents can reach), `guards` (steps added for every level up to the person's), `climb` (steps added when this is the next level).
- `sources`: `{id, label, url}`; `basis: true` marks the research behind the scale itself, the rest back single numbers and cases. Both lists show under "What this is based on".
- `parts`: `level`, `risks` and `around`. A part with `optional: true` is offered after the result instead of being asked in the main check.
- The level: one question with `place: true`, one tap, five options with a `level`. It asks for the furthest the person went in the last month, even once. Guardrails and their questions apply for every level up to it. Logic is `levelOf()` in `index.html` and `level_of()` in `exposure.py`.
- `questions`: each option can `add` step ids to the plan or mark them `done`, and can carry a `note` with its `src`. `showIf` hides a question, `multi` makes it multi-select, `exclusive` marks an option like "None", `effects` add steps whenever the question is answered (optionally with `if`). `covers: n` says which level's guardrails the question checks; the question screen shows it as "Guardrails for level n". Every question has a `part`.
- `steps`: `level` (0 = around you, 1–5 = where the risk starts or, with `up`, the level the step gets you to), `up` (a level-up step), `why` (one line, shown for essential steps), `minutes`, `impact` (1–3, used for ordering, not shown), `tier` (1 essential, 2 recommended, 3 advanced), `checkMethod` (how a full build could check it: auto/link/self; not shown), `withOthers` (needs family, a bank or a company), `links`, `agentHelp` (one line on what the skill does for this step; `exposure.py validate` fails if the skill has no check, template or review for it). A link is `{label, url, showIf?}`.
- **Cover** (`coverOf()`, `cover()`): for each level up to the person's, take the tier-1 steps of that level that are in the plan and count how many are in place. The verdict on the result screen is the sum over those levels. Level 0 gives the same count for "Around you".
- **First step** (`firstStep()`): the step to do next, among steps to do at levels 1 to the person's: essentials before anything else, then higher impact, then the higher level, then the shorter time.
- Conditions (`showIf`, `if`): `{question, equals | notEquals | minCount}` or `{minLevel}` (level at least n). `minCount` ignores exclusive options. The level question can't use `minLevel`.
- Ids are stored in visitors' `localStorage` (key `exposure-scale-v3`: answers, ticks, and whether the optional part was started). Renaming a question, option value or step id resets that answer for returning visitors. A deploy doesn't reset anything; to reset everyone after a material change, bump the key and remove the old one on load, as the page does for `exposure-scale-v2`. `localStorage` belongs to the whole `yaedin.github.io` origin, shared with any other Pages project there, so only ever remove this app's own keys, never `localStorage.clear()`.
- Start over (on every question screen and under the result) asks once, then removes the key.
- **Screens** (no tabs; `render()` picks one): start (`vStart`: statement, lede, Start, "Use your AI", the five levels as a rail), question (`vQuestion`: which level or part it belongs to, progress segments, the reply to the last answer, the question), result (`vResult`), and `vUnplaced` when both level questions were skipped.
- **Reply** (`replyHTML`): after each answer, what it put in place or added (step titles, not counts), the option's note with its source link, and the level's state once its questions are done.
- **Result** (`vResult`): left, the verdict ("3 of your 8 guardrails are in place"), one line on the level ("You go up to level 4"), and the first step with its why, the answer that put it there, its links and "It's in place". Right, the five levels as a rail, one open at a time (`S.lvOpen`; by default the level of the first step). A level up to the person's shows its count ("1 of 2 in place") and opens to its risk, its case with the source, the echo, its essential steps, and the rest behind "More steps" (steps in the plan first, then the level's other steps, which replaces the old All steps catalog). The level above yours shows how to get there, its climb steps and the guardrails it needs. Levels beyond show only their line. Below the rail: "Around you" (an offer to answer its questions, then a group like a level), then Copy result as text (`resultText()`, built in the browser), Your answers (tap one to change it), What this is based on, Start over.
- Question order: the level (one question), then guardrail questions level by level up to it, then, only if the person asks, around you (voice clones, teens, likeness, posts, decisions).

## Use your AI (chat prompt)
- The start screen has one "Use your AI" link next to Start. It opens a panel: three plain lines on what the prompt does (`intro.aiLines`), "Show the full prompt", then ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copy prompt. Each app link copies the prompt and opens the app; only ChatGPT is prefilled, through `chatgpt.com/#?q=` (the part after # stays in the browser), because a long query string risks a "URL too long" error elsewhere. The panel exists so nobody runs a prompt they haven't seen (EXE-55). Copy is `intro.ai`, `intro.aiLines` and `intro.copied`.
- `buildPrompt()` in `index.html` builds it from `content.json` in two parts: a short block a person can read in seconds, then a marked reference (`PROMPT_REF`) with how to score (level, the guardrail count, the first step), the levels with guardrails and climb steps, the questions with what each option adds (+id) or marks in place (✓id), and every step with its links and whether it is essential. The level is the only question asked upfront; the model infers the rest from memory and past chats, with evidence, and asks what it couldn't tell at the end. No link back to the site and no consent step in the chat (decided Nov 2026, EXE-49, EXE-55).
- `node tools/check_prompt.mjs` checks coverage, the 16,000-character limit and the 1,200-character limit on the readable part. Run it after changing `content.json` or the prompt. The prompt sits about 500 characters under the limit, so new content soon means trimming elsewhere.

## Agent skill
`skills/exposure-scale/` is a terminal version for coding agents (Agent Skills standard: `SKILL.md` plus `scripts/`, `assets/`, `references/`).
- `scripts/exposure.py` (Python 3.8+, standard library only) holds the logic: questions, cover, plan, step help, read-only checks, state in `~/.exposure-scale/state.json`. The agent relays its output; `SKILL.md` sets the rules (never change settings, never handle secrets, follow the `Next:` line). It asks the optional questions too, marked `(optional)`; `SKILL.md` tells the agent to offer them first.
- `assets/content.json` is a copy of the site's `content.json`; `tools/check_content.py` fails if they differ. Terminal-only help per step (how-to text, phone instructions, templates) lives in `assets/assist.json`.
- `node tools/compare_logic.mjs` checks that the script and `index.html` give the same questions, plan, links, level, cover and first step on random answers, and draws every screen for each answer set so a rendering error fails too. Run it after changing logic in either.
- `python3 skills/exposure-scale/scripts/exposure.py validate` checks `assist.json`, probes, templates and the `SKILL.md` header.
- `evals/exposure-scale.json` holds scenarios to rerun on several models after changing `SKILL.md`. They were rewritten for this version and have not been rerun.
- `.claude/skills/`, `.agents/skills/` and `.gemini/skills/` hold relative symlinks to `skills/exposure-scale` so agents opened in this repo pick it up. Edit the real folder, not the links.
- Windows and Linux checks are untested.

## Checking content
`python3 tools/check_content.py` checks that every id in `content.json` resolves (steps, questions, sources), that every note with a number names a source, that exactly one question sets the level, that every essential guardrail has an answer that marks it in place, and that every link responds, including the sources. Sites that block scripts (FTC consumer pages, Facebook, ICO) are listed for a browser check. Links behind a sign-in only prove the page exists; whether they land on the right setting needs a signed-in check. Update `linksChecked` after a full pass.

3. **Impressum.** Deferred by the owner. Ask before adding anything.
4. Content is US/EU-focused; other regions only get generic steps.
5. **The level mapping is a reading, not a measurement.** No study measures risk per level for individuals, and which steps count as essential (tier 1) is a judgment. The count rests on self-report: one answer per essential guardrail. Whether people find the result more useful than the old level is untested; that is the question for the next round of user tests.
6. **Open decisions for the owner (Nov 2026):** the footer line (`intro.by`) says "Made by one person, with AI" and could name the author instead, which the personal-site brief favours; the basis paragraph mentions the ten user tests in the first person (`intro.basis`), which is new public wording. The font could move to Schibsted Grotesk to match the personal site.
7. **Candidates, not yet added** (narrowness first): AI meeting notetakers recording you, AI features reading your email (Gmail smart features), AI on by default in phones and laptops (Apple Intelligence, Windows Recall).

## Research behind the content (as of Oct–Nov 2026)
- Cases and numbers shown on the page, each opened on 16 November 2026 (ids in `sources`): shared ChatGPT chats indexed by Google, option removed 1 Aug 2025 (`chat_index`); chatbots affirm the user 49% more often than people, 11 models, Science, Mar 2026 (`sycophancy`; earlier notes here said "about 50%"); 66% of office workers used AI tools they believed weren't allowed, PagerDuty/Wakefield, n=1,250, Jun 2026 (`pagerduty`); survey of 319 knowledge workers, more confidence in AI goes with less critical thinking, Lee et al., CHI 2025 (`critical_thinking`); Deloitte Australia partial refund on an AU$440,000 report, Oct 2025 (`deloitte`); Charlotin's database listed 2,293 cases on 16 Nov 2026, shown as "more than 2,200" (`charlotin`); adenoma detection without AI fell from 28.4% to 22.4% after AI was introduced, observational, four Polish centres (`deskilling`); EchoLeak, CVE-2025-32711, a zero-click prompt injection in Microsoft 365 Copilot, fixed, no known exploitation (`echoleak`); Replit agent deleted a production database, Jul 2025 (`replit`); Joseph Cox got into his own Lloyds account with a cloned voice, Feb 2023 (`voice_id`). Update the Charlotin count when it moves.
- Levels: Feng, McDonald & Zhang, "Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents" (2025): operator, collaborator, consultant, approver, observer. DeepMind "Levels of AGI" autonomy levels (tool → agent). Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025: three phases (assistant; agents as digital colleagues at human direction; agents run whole processes, humans check in). Dan Shapiro's five levels for coding (Jan 2026) are a social signal for the same ladder.
- Anthropic internal use ("How employment is changing at Anthropic", Oct 2026): agents passed human work hours in March 2026; standing agent instructions went from a few hundred to 8,500 in a year; an EA built an assistant agent and moved to chief-of-staff work; 20% describe shifting from doing work to managing Claudes.
- Risk rises with autonomy: Mitchell et al. (Hugging Face, 2025). Anthropic "Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice" (Feb 2026): full auto-approve rises from ~20% (new) to 40%+ (750+ sessions) of Claude Code sessions; 0.8% of API agent actions look irreversible.
- Level 2: two in three office workers have used AI they believed their employer prohibits (PagerDuty 2026). Higher trust in AI goes with less critical thinking (Lee et al., CHI 2025).
- Level 3: about 2,000 court cases with AI-invented citations (Charlotin database, Aug 2026: 1,981). METR (2025): developers felt 20% faster while measured 19% slower; self-assessment is unreliable, which is why placement asks about behavior.
- Level 4: the lethal trifecta (Willison, 2025): private data + untrusted content + a way to send. Scheduled tasks exist in Claude Cowork, ChatGPT and Gemini.
- Level 5: Replit's agent deleted a production database during a code freeze (Jul 2025). OpenClaw (Jan–Feb 2026): tens of thousands of exposed instances and hundreds of malicious skills on ClawHub.
- LinkedIn trains on user data by default (setting: Data privacy → Data for Generative AI Improvement). GitHub opted personal Copilot accounts into training in April 2026.
- Voice cloning: a few seconds of audio can be enough; bank voice ID has been beaten by clones; family safe word + callback is the main defense. Voice "cloaking" tools (AntiFake) can be stripped by purification (De-AntiFake, ICML 2025), so they're not recommended.
- YouTube likeness detection is open to all creators 18+ since May 2026 (face only, flags but doesn't remove). Loti offers free likeness monitoring.
- US TAKE IT DOWN Act: platforms must remove nonconsensual intimate images, including deepfakes, within 48 hours; FTC enforcement since 19 May 2026 (TakeItDown.ftc.gov).
- Teens: 72% of US teens have used AI companions (Common Sense Media, Jul 2025). Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s in Nov 2025; Meta paused teen access to AI characters in Jan 2026; ChatGPT parental controls link a parent and teen account but don't show chats; Snapchat lets parents disable My AI; Claude is 18+. California SB 243 (Jan 2026) and New York's companion law require disclosure and crisis protocols.
- Sycophancy: models back the user about 50% more often than people (Stanford, Science, Mar 2026). Instructions help but don't remove it; asking for the strongest case against works better than "don't flatter me" (UK AISI "Ask Don't Tell", 2026).
- AI training on creative work: DeviantArt and ArtStation opt out by default; X trains Grok on posts by default. Training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Meta-ExternalAgent; Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended are control tokens) say they honour robots.txt; user-triggered fetchers often don't.
- Automated decisions: GDPR Art. 22 and 15(1)(h) give a right to human review and an explanation (CJEU C-203/22, Feb 2025). UK replaced Art. 22 with Arts. 22A–D from 5 Feb 2026. California ADMT rules apply from 1 Jan 2027; Colorado's replacement law from 1 Jan 2027. Whether EU AI Act Art. 86 applies from Aug 2026 or Dec 2027 is unclear.
- Face search engines (PimEyes, FaceCheck.ID) make faces searchable; PimEyes has an opt-out form.
- Prompt injection is the main risk of AI agents acting in logged-in sessions; no complete defense exists.
- Prior art: Consumer Reports Security Planner, EFF Surveillance Self-Defense. Neither covers AI exposure much; that's this tool's gap.
